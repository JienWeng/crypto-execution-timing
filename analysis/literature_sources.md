# Full-paper literature update

The full-paper library contains **40 research papers and one Binance documentation record**. All 40 research papers are cited in substantive paragraphs in `manuscript/full/literature.tex` (approximately 1,145 words excluding citation commands). The documentation belongs with the data description. The library includes explicitly labelled working papers and preprints; this is a targeted literature synthesis, not a systematic review or a claim that every record is peer reviewed.

The author's earlier preprint is excluded from the new library and literature section at the author's request. One published paper replaces that record, keeping the research-paper count at 40:

- Maciel, Leandro S. (2025). *Multifractality and the economic value of equity risk premium forecasts*. Applied Economics Letters. Published online 27 April 2025. DOI: [10.1080/13504851.2025.2498060](https://doi.org/10.1080/13504851.2025.2498060).
- Primary verification: [publisher abstract and metadata](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2025.2498060?af=R), inspected 19 September 2026; corroborating [author-institution repository record](https://repositorio.usp.br/item/003253990).
- Evidence boundary: abstract and metadata, not a full-text replication. The manuscript uses only the supported statement that the source evaluates equity risk-premium forecasts through portfolio allocation. It does not transfer its reported empirical results to cryptocurrency execution or claim a shared estimator.

The remaining 39 papers retain the prior verified metadata and narrow claim boundaries. Their source-by-source audit is preserved in `revisions/2026-09-19/literature_update.md`. The new section draws on the previously inspected passages for the closest theoretical comparators (Lehalle–Neuman, Forde–Sánchez-Betancourt–Smith, Abi Jaber–Neuman) and the uncertainty gate (Irshad–Biswas). It explicitly acknowledges that signal-aware execution, relative-price contrasts and uncertainty-based abstention precede this study.

The new synthesis distinguishes: (i) terminal-return scoring from schedule-relevant price profiles; (ii) schedule calibration from general decision-focused learning; (iii) uncertainty about the signal from misspecified impact; (iv) predictive coverage from economic guarantees; and (v) bar-data replay from executable order-book strategies. It does not claim that an elementary fixed-volume identity is novel or that bootstrap calibration inherits guarantees from conformal or safe reinforcement-learning methods.
