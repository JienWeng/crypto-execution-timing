## Closest prior work and required positioning

The general ideas that execution should use predictive information, that prediction error and decision loss differ, and that uncertain signals can be ignored or attenuated are established. The revision should claim a **transparent finite-grid diagnostic and controlled empirical comparison of terminal-direction scores with execution-relevant profile scores**, conditional on its actual experiments. The centered-price identity is useful exposition; it is not a new optimal-execution principle.

### Precise mathematical boundary (our reasoning)

If all feasible schedules satisfy 1′u = Q and the forecast enters a fixed objective linearly as −u′p, replacing p by p+c1 adds the same −cQ to every schedule. The optimizer therefore does not change when all other costs and constraints stay fixed. For two equal-volume schedules δ = u−v, δ′p = δ′Mp, with M = I−11′/N. Only the centered profile affects their linear price comparison.

This is a **common price-level shift**, not a constant drift shift. Constant drift generally changes expected prices differently across execution slots and can alter the optimizer. The identity also does not justify invariance of a ratio normalized by a changing arrival price, nor of forecast-dependent constraints or impact parameters. A terminal forecast alone does not identify the centered profile: equal terminal expected returns can be generated by different intrahorizon paths. A jump completed before the first executable slot gives a particularly clear constant-level example, but is a constructed timing argument rather than a universal description of BTC price discovery.

A defensible contribution statement is: “We use an elementary fixed-volume decomposition to distinguish terminal-return predictability from the temporal price contrasts that change an execution schedule, and evaluate both under a fully specified chronological cryptocurrency replay.” Add claims about robustness or improvement only if the revised results support them. Report the replay as conditional on the execution proxy and assumed impact, rather than realized achievable savings.

## Synthesis for supporting material

Predictive execution predates this work: the foundational impact–risk problem (`almgren2001`) was extended to order-flow and signal information (`cartea2016`, `bechler2015`, `cartea2018`, `lehalle2019`), learning (`casgrain2019`) and adaptive transient-impact controls (`neuman2022`). Remaining-horizon price contrasts already occur in `forde2022` and `abijaber2025`; predictor persistence also matters in the portfolio-choice setting of `garleanu2013`. The recent trade-signal formulation `bank2026` further rules out broad novelty claims about conditioning execution on forecasts. The starting preprint `lai2026` should be acknowledged separately as the source of inherited material.

Robustness is likewise an existing research direction. Model uncertainty (`cartea2017`), liquidation ambiguity (`cartea_liquidation2017`), factor uncertainty (`horst2022`), signal thresholds (`demarch2018`), impact misspecification (`hey2023`), pessimistic impact learning (`neumanoffline2023`) and stochastic liquidity/impact (`fouque2022`, `macri2024`) address different sources of error. Portfolio estimation uncertainty (`kan2007`) and safe baseline policy improvement (`laroche2019`) are useful context but do not supply guarantees for our rule. `irshad2026` is the closest recent execution uncertainty gate and must appear explicitly in the comparison.

The distinction between prediction and decision quality is central to `elmachtoub2022`, calibration in `liu2021`, the review `mandi2024`, directional surrogates `gupta2024` and dependence-aware preprint `liudependent2024`. Our centered-profile score is a task-specific diagnostic within this established motivation, not a general replacement for decision-focused learning. If conformal inference is discussed, separate exchangeability from adaptive coverage: `gibbs2021`, `barber2023`, `zaffran2022` and `xu2021` establish different results under different conditions. None justifies labelling an empirical shrinkage coefficient a distribution-free safety guarantee.

Crypto-specific relevance comes from real execution work (`bundi2024`, `schnaubelt2022`), impact analysis (`barucci2023`), market segmentation (`makarov2020`) and the microstructure survey (`almeida2024`). These sources motivate exchange-, horizon- and data-resolution-specific conclusions. Dependence and repeated model choice remain separate risks: `politis1994` motivates a particular bootstrap family, while `bailey2017` and `arnott2019` motivate selection disclosure and disciplined chronology. An expanded test designed after inspecting an initial month is exploratory unless a genuinely untouched evaluation period and decision protocol were locked prospectively.
